# Methods: how the figure panels are computed

Applies to both figure sets. "Native" = each method at its sequenced read length (BOBseq 2 x 150 nt paired-end; DRUG-seq 52 nt, prime-seq 50 nt single-end). "50 nt" = every method reduced to one 50-nt single read per fragment (BOBseq: read 2, the template-switch end, truncated to 50 nt). Alignment, gene assignment and every downstream rule are identical between methods and sets; only the input reads differ. The benchmark compares DRUG-seq, prime-seq and BOBseq (the method list is `benchmark/scripts/palette.py`, METH).

## Data and alignment

Public data: DRUG-seq (GSE176150 / SRR14730306, U-2 OS, DMSO wells), prime-seq (E-MTAB-10142 / PRJEB43323, HEK293T, "Incubation + ProtK" 10k-cell samples); BOBseq: the 24-plex HEK293T library (18 benchmark samples: six treatments in triplicate; the three risdiplam 500 nM wells are excluded as input failures and the three RAW 264.7 mouse wells serve only as species controls). Reads were aligned with STAR (identical parameters for every arm, `benchmark/scripts/settings.sh`) to a combined GRCh38.113 + GRCm39.113 reference with Ensembl 113 annotation; multimappers were kept in the BAM. Each mapped primary read was classified once into a canonical read table (well, UMI, contig, 5' anchor position, unique/multimapping, region class, gene): T = mitochondrial contig; R = rRNA/rDNA; otherwise the gene with the largest overlap: E = exonic protein-coding, X = exonic other biotype, I = intronic, G = intergenic. Every panel is a filter on this table (or on the BAM for coverage and junctions), so no arm has its own policy.

**Benchmark samples.** DRUG-seq: the 24 DMSO wells of the plate (raw well barcodes mapped to the whitelist); prime-seq: 8 HEK293T samples; BOBseq: 18 samples (Hek control 2 included; its TSO oligo carries a T-poor UMI mix, which the composition-aware UMI rule absorbs). All library-level and per-sample numbers use exactly these wells.

**Filtered read.** A uniquely mapped read (STAR NH = 1, MAPQ 255) assigned to an exon of a protein-coding gene (class E) on a human contig. "Filtered reads per sample" is this count per well.

## p0a. Read fate

Line plot through the steps reads into STAR, mapped (unique + multimapping), uniquely mapped, filtered, each as % of that well's reads into STAR: line = mean over the benchmark wells of a method, shaded ribbon = 95% t-interval of the mean, in-plot legend with the number of wells; per-well values in values/. Per-well counts of the first three steps come from the pooled STAR BAM (unmapped reads retained, sample tag in the read name; raw DRUG-seq barcodes mapped to wells) and, for BOBseq, from the pipeline's per-well input and mapping tables; filtered reads from the per-well read table. Read-name barcodes and well lists are the same as for every other panel.

## p0c and p0d2. Filtered reads per sample after de-duplication, duplicate rate at matched depth

Per benchmark well at native depth (no subsampling): filtered reads (unique, exonic protein-coding, as sequenced) and molecules = UCI, the same unique (UMI, gene) count with the over-use filter and collision correction used in p1. p0c: molecules per sample after de-duplication, one dot per well, bar = median, log axis. Duplicate rate = 100 x (1 - molecules / filtered reads) per well. The as-sequenced rate depends on depth (BOBseq wells carry about ten times the filtered reads of a competitor sample) and is not a figure panel; p0d2 reports the rate with every well subsampled to 250k filtered reads (from the p1 rarefaction; wells below 250k are omitted), which is the like-for-like comparison. Values per sample in values/p0c_p0d_dedup_per_sample_*.tsv. Note that for BOBseq this is the benchmark's read-table definition (filtered reads only, 8-nt UMI); the pipeline's own deduplication of all mapped fragments (position, fragment length and UMI) gives a different absolute rate.

## p1. Molecules per sample vs depth (rarefaction)

For every well, filtered reads are subsampled without replacement to 50k, 100k, 250k, 500k, 1M and 2M reads (uniform reservoir; smaller depths are prefixes of one shuffled reservoir). At each depth, molecules are counted as unique (UMI, gene) pairs per well with a composition-aware over-use filter (UMI tags used more than 8x their base-composition expectation, and homopolymer tags, are removed) and a collision correction for the short-UMI arms (skew-aware estimator, the "B_corr" or UCI value used throughout the benchmark). Wells with fewer filtered reads than a depth are not shown at that depth (rarefaction only goes down). Thick line = mean over wells at each depth, shaded ribbon = 95% t-interval of the mean (t-distribution, depths with at least 3 wells), linear axis from zero, in-plot legend with the number of wells. The per-well values behind the line are in values/. A log-axis version (p1_molecules_vs_depth_log) is drawn beside the linear one. Molecules and genes are counted on the same subsampled read set, so an arm sequenced deeper cannot look better for that reason.

## p2. Genes per sample at matched depth

Same subsampling and molecule filter as p1; a gene is counted when at least one kept molecule maps to it. Ribosomal-protein genes are not counted: gene symbols matching RPL, RPS, RPLP, MRPL or MRPS, with the RPS6K kinases spared, the same rule as the composition and coverage panels. Only the gene count changes: the subsampling depth (filtered reads) and the molecule count of p1 still include reads of those genes. The values table carries both counts (genes, genes_incl_ribosomal_protein). p2 strips: one dot per well at the stated depth, bar = median. p2 curves: mean, ribbon and legend as p1.

## p3. Read composition

The classification rules of the preprint, applied per benchmark sample to the per-sample human BAMs (primary mapped records, multimappers included; native BOBseq mate 1 only, one record per fragment as in the pipeline) by `composition_rules.py`; first match wins on the primary alignment: rRNA (span overlaps an rDNA locus, or an aligned block overlaps an exon of an rRNA / Mt_rRNA / rRNA_pseudogene gene), mitochondrial (contig MT), ribosomal-protein mRNA (aligned block overlaps an exon of an RPL/RPS/MRPL/MRPS/RPLP gene), mRNA (aligned block overlaps an exon of any other protein-coding gene), exonic other biotype, intronic (span inside a gene, no exon overlap), intergenic. Stacked shares of mapped reads, summed over the benchmark wells of each method. A read-table version (classes E, X, I, G, R, T, max-overlap gene priority) was computed as a check; it under-counts mRNA by 4-9 points because of the gene priority, while rRNA and intronic agree within 0.2 points.

## p3b. Intronic reads per sample

The intronic class of p3 per benchmark well (same rules and BAMs): intronic reads as a percentage of all mapped reads of that well, multimappers included. Wells: the benchmark set of every method (DRUG-seq 24, prime-seq 8, BOBseq 18; the three risdiplam 500 nM wells excluded as input failures, as in p1 and p2). One dot per well, bar = median.

## p4. Coverage along the transcript

**Transcript model.** One transcript per protein-coding gene: the Ensembl canonical transcript (MANE Select where defined), exons only; length L = mature-transcript length. Every unique (MAPQ 255) primary read is placed on this transcript by its 5' end (t = distance from the transcript 5' end, 0..L-1, strand-aware), with its aligned length; reads outside canonical exons are ignored. For native BOBseq both mates are placed (one fragment paints both mate alignments); for the 50-nt set every method has one 50-nt read per fragment. Samples: every benchmark sample (18 BOBseq, 24 DRUG-seq, 8 prime-seq; Ris 500 nM excluded), pooled per method.

**Gene set and subsampling.** Genes with at least 200 unique reads in every method (2,492 native, 2,486 in the 50-nt set); for each gene and method exactly 200 reads are drawn at random (seed fixed), so depth and expression are identical between methods for every gene. Per-base coverage of the transcript is the number of drawn reads overlapping each base.

**Heatmap layout (p4i2).** One row per gene, sorted by transcript length (short at top); 50 equal bins from 5' to 3'; each row divided by its own maximum. Same genes and same order in the three panels.

**p4m2 unscaled profile.** Genes of the set with L >= 2 kb (1,435 native / 1,432 in the 50-nt set). For each gene the per-base coverage is divided by the gene's mean coverage (1 = uniform), then the last 3,000 nt are aligned at the annotated 3' end (poly(A) site = 0) and the profiles are averaged over genes with equal weight (a 25-nt running mean is applied for display only). Genes shorter than 3 kb contribute where they exist. No scaling to transcript length, so a 3'-end method cannot look even by having a short window stretched over the axis.

## p4 threshold panels (p4i2 and p4m2, ">= 1 kb, no RP")

Same transcript model, unique reads and gene selection as p4, with two gene cutoffs: canonical transcript at least 1,000 nt and ribosomal-protein genes (RPL, RPS, MRPL, MRPS, RPLP; the RPS6K kinases are not ribosomal proteins and stay) excluded; no fragment-length rule (a 500-nt rule on the per-read aligned length removes nothing at 150 nt). The heatmap uses all unique reads of every gene; the poly(A)-anchored profile uses 200 reads per gene (seed 11), coverage divided by the gene mean, the mean over genes at each distance from the poly(A) site; genes shorter than the 3,000-nt window contribute where they exist, so the axis is fully populated only up to 1,000 nt (the number of contributing genes at 1, 2 and 3 kb is in p4_thresholds_numbers.json and in values/).

## p6. Picard gene-body coverage and 5' to 3' balance

Picard CollectRnaSeqMetrics (3.5.0, STRAND_SPECIFICITY NONE, MINIMUM_LENGTH 1000) per benchmark sample on the per-sample human BAMs of the set (primary mapped records of the benchmark alignments, raw reads, native BOBseq with both mates), against a refFlat of the protein-coding Ensembl 113 transcripts with ribosomal-protein and mitochondrial genes removed (bare chromosome names, `build_refflat_filtered.py`). Profile = mean over samples of the per-sample 101-bin normalised coverage, ribbon = 95% t-interval. Balance = 2 x sum(p_i c_i) / sum(c_i) over the 101 bins with p_i in [0, 1]: 1 = even, below 1 = 5'-leaning, above 1 = 3'-leaning; one dot per sample, bar = median. Metrics files are kept under coverage_architecture/picard_metrics/ of the working directory; the validation re-runs Picard on one DRUG-seq and one BOBseq sample and requires identical histograms.

## p7. Read length, insert length, aligned bases

**p7a read length.** Aligned length (matched bases of the primary alignment, exon-aware) of every unique (MAPQ 255) read on a canonical protein-coding transcript, from the same position tables as the coverage panels; BOBseq split by mate (mate 1 = read 2 after removal of the bobcode, UMI and TSO at prep; mate 2 = read 1). DRUG-seq and prime-seq reads are their native 52 and 50 nt.

**p7e and p7f aligned bases.** Per sample, the sum of the aligned length over its unique reads on canonical protein-coding transcripts (the p7a reads; BOBseq both mates), i.e. read number x mean aligned length, as sequenced (p7e, filled dots, Gb) and at a matched depth of 250k filtered reads (open dots: 250k x the sample's aligned bases per fragment; exact, because the per-fragment value does not depend on depth), and p7f, not a figure panel, the same sum divided by the number of unique fragments (BOBseq: the mate-1 records, one per pair). Per-sample values in values/p7e_p7f_aligned_bases_per_sample_*.tsv.

**p7b insert length (BOBseq only).** From the deduplicated pair BAMs of the 18 benchmark wells: for every pair whose two mates are unique primary alignments on the same canonical transcript, the insert length on the mature transcript = |t(5' end of mate 1) - t(5' end of mate 2)| + 1, where t is the transcript coordinate of a read's 5' end (each mate's 5' end is one end of the cDNA fragment; because t is exon-aware, introns between the mates do not inflate it, unlike the genomic template length, which is kept beside it in the values). Pooled histogram over the 18 wells; the per-well medians are the insert-length row of the stacked QC table. The competitors are single-end, so no insert length is observable for them. Scripts: library_metrics.py (readlen, insert), library_panels.py.

## Raw values

`values/` holds one table per panel: per sample (p0a, p0c/p0d2, p1, p2, p3b, p6b, p7e, the funnel levels), per method (p3, the pooled junction curves), per gene x bin (heatmap), per nucleotide (profiles) and per metric x sample (stacked QC). p1 and p2 also have a *_mean_ci95.tsv with, per method and depth, the number of wells, mean, SD and the 95% t-interval of the mean (the thick line and the ribbon). The numbers are the reference; the panels are drawings of them (replot.py redraws every panel from values/). `README.md` of this directory maps every panel to its table.

## p5. Unique splice junctions vs depth (minimum 3 molecules)

Source: the per-sample junction BEDs of `extract_junctions.sh` (shipped as `data/junctions/`), computed on the per-sample human BAMs of this benchmark after UMI-deduplication (UMI + position; BOBseq 6-nt UMI, DRUG-seq and prime-seq 10 nt). Junction support = deduplicated molecules with MAPQ 255; a pair spanning a junction counts once. A junction is identified by its intron coordinates (the strand is not used). Rarefaction: every sample is subsampled to 25k, 50k, 100k, 250k, 500k, 1M and 2M deduplicated molecules; subsampling thins each junction's count binomially with p = depth / the sample's molecule total, so the expected number of junctions with at least 3 molecules at a depth is the sum over junctions of P(Binom(n, p) >= 3), exact in expectation without touching the BAMs. Samples with fewer molecules than a depth are omitted there (DRUG-seq wells hold 87k to 300k molecules, so their curve ends at 250k with 10 wells). Thick line = mean over samples, ribbon = 95% t-interval. The x axis is deduplicated molecules, not the filtered reads of p1 and p2. The generator also writes the curves for thresholds 1, 2, 5 and 10 (working directory only).

**Four variants (minimum 3 molecules per junction, native set).** (1) p5_junctions_vs_depth_min3: the panel above, all junctions. (2) p5_annotated_junctions_vs_depth_min3: a junction counts only when its intron (chromosome, start, end) is an intron of a transcript in the Ensembl 113 annotation the reads were aligned to (528,735 human introns, all transcripts and biotypes); the coordinate convention was verified (75% of a BOBseq sample's junctions match at the stated offset, under 1% at any shifted offset). About 96 to 97% of junction molecules of every method fall on annotated junctions, so from 2 molecules upward the annotated and all-junction curves coincide; at 250k molecules and minimum 3 the BOBseq mean moves from 5,113 to 5,100. (3) p5_pooled_annotated_junctions_vs_depth_min3 and (4) p5_pooled_all_junctions_vs_depth_min3: dataset level. Per method the molecule count of every junction is summed over its benchmark samples (DRUG-seq 24, prime-seq 8, BOBseq 18) and the pool is thinned binomially to 25k to 100M molecules, the last point being the full pool (DRUG-seq 5.4 M, prime-seq 7.9 M, BOBseq 62.3 M molecules). Pooling exposes that the competitors keep re-detecting the same junctions: BOBseq over DRUG-seq at minimum 3 is 7.2x per sample at 250k, 12.1x pooled at 5 M and 30.8x at the full pools; over prime-seq 3.1x, 2.5x and 5.1x (annotated). Full pools at minimum 3: BOBseq 115,256 annotated junctions, prime-seq 22,611, DRUG-seq 3,746. Generators: junction_rarefaction.py, junction_rarefaction_annotated.py, junction_rarefaction_pooled.py; values/ has one TSV per panel (per sample and depth, or per method and pooled depth).

## Gene examples (not shipped: regenerate with benchmark/scripts/gene_coverage_tracks.py, or view the loci in a genome browser)

Browser-style coverage figures: one filled track per method with the peak depth as the only y tick, the canonical transcript below (exons as boxes on the intron line), hg38 positions under it, the gene drawn 5' to 3' from left to right. Two versions per gene: genomic (introns to scale) and exons only (introns removed, exons joined to scale, ticks = hg38 position of exon starts). Data: the pooled depth-matched BAMs (see below), so peak heights are comparable between methods; coverage = read depth over aligned blocks. The generator writes, per gene, a <gene>_depth.tsv with the depth per base and method; replot.py draws the tracks from such tables when they are placed in values/. Full-coverage examples: RELA, MLF2, BAG6, TUBG1, EEF2, MCM7, GIGYF1; prime-seq intronic examples: GRB2, IRS4, FUS, PPP5C. Candidates were ranked by gene_peakiness_scan.py (BOBseq completeness: share of the transcript covered, share of positions at 1/4 of the mean depth or more; prime-seq: sharp peaks, intronic fragment share and intronic depth outside any annotated exon). Generator: gene_coverage_tracks.py.

**Pooled depth-matched tracks.** For each method the per-sample human BAMs of its benchmark samples were pooled and exactly 8.0 M uniquely mapped fragments kept (MAPQ 255, primary; a BOBseq pair = one fragment, both mates kept), chosen by a 64-bit hash of sample and read name, so the choice is deterministic and uniform; contigs are the primary chromosomes with UCSC names. Pooled unique fragments before matching: DRUG-seq 12.6 M, prime-seq 8.5 M (the cap), BOBseq 100.7 M. A second set keeps every unique fragment with bigWigs scaled per 10 M. Generator: ucsc_pooled_tracks.py.

**prime-seq peaks.** The sharp, narrow coverage peaks of prime-seq (the highest peak of each of 1,892 genes, at least 1 kb from the 3' end) have base composition within 60 nt indistinguishable from random exonic positions (an A-rich stretch within 60 nt in 9% of peaks vs 5% background), are made of distinct molecules (0.92 distinct sample + UMI per read, as in control windows) and are shared across all eight libraries (the largest library contributes a median 22% of a peak). They are a reproducible positional preference of the prime-seq library preparation, not internal poly(A) priming or PCR duplication. Scripts: primeseq_peak_sequences.py, primeseq_peak_umis.py.

## Fig. S6B: stacked per-sample QC figure (stacked_qc_all_samples)

One table of 14 metrics as rows and 10 columns: DRUG-seq (24 DMSO wells), prime-seq (8 samples), the six BOBseq benchmark treatments (3 replicates each), the three risdiplam 500 nM wells (input failures, grey) and the three mouse wells (RAW 264.7, own colour, set apart). One dot per sample, short horizontal line = mean (geometric mean on log rows); triplicates are drawn replicate 1, 2, 3 from left to right, larger columns as a compact symmetric swarm. Rows: reads into STAR, mapping rate, uniquely mapped, filtered reads (% of reads into STAR), filtered reads after de-duplication (molecules), duplicate rate at 250k filtered reads, genes at 250k filtered reads (ribosomal-protein genes excluded), rRNA, mRNA, intronic and mitochondrial reads (% of mapped, classification rules of p3), insert length median (BOBseq only, the competitors are single-end), aligned bases, 5'-3' balance. Matched-depth rows use the p1 rarefaction; the three DRUG-seq wells with fewer than 250k filtered reads are not subsampled and are marked with a grey cross on the baseline. Sources are the primary tables behind the main panels (funnel and per-well tables, rarefied.tsv, composition counts, position tables, Picard metrics); validate_new_outputs.py recomputes every plotted value from them. Generator: stacked_supplement_merged.py; the values table lists every dot.

## Coverage vs distance from the poly(A) site, all 24 samples (S14; not in the manuscript)

The 21 human wells (treatment colours; risdiplam 500 nM dashed) and the 3 mouse wells (purple) in one panel. One rule for every well, human or mouse: canonical protein-coding transcripts of at least 1 kb, ribosomal-protein genes excluded, genes with at least 20 unique reads in that well; per gene, coverage divided by the gene mean, anchored at the 3' end, 3-kb window, mean over genes, 25-nt smoothing; y range 0 to 8.5 as in p4m2. The benchmark panel p4m2 uses instead the gene set shared by the three methods; on a HEK well the two rules differ by at most 0.1. Generator: supplement_polyA_24.py.

## Mouse wells

The three RAW 264.7 wells of the 24-plex went through the same arm path as the human wells (mate-1 records of the raw pair BAMs, read table, filtered-read slice, molecule counting, per-sample BAM, positions on canonical transcripts, insert length, Picard) with the mouse half of the combined reference (GRCm39, Ensembl 113; species switch BM_SPECIES_PREFIX=MOUSE_ in coverage_lib.py, composition_rules.py and library_metrics.py, default human). Filtered read = uniquely mapped, exon of a protein-coding gene, on a mouse contig. Read composition = the classification rules on the mouse-contig records plus the well's records on the human rDNA loci counted as rRNA: GRCm39 carries almost no rDNA, so the aligner places mouse rRNA on the human rDNA copies of the combined reference (about 15% of a mouse well's primary reads); the few other human-contig records (0.6%) are left out, as mouse-contig records are for the human wells. Picard uses a mouse refFlat built with the rule of the human one. Genes at 250k count mouse genes. Scripts: mouse3_build_wells.py, mouse3_tables.py, mouse3_native_bams.py (run by run_coverage.sh); the mouse wells appear in the stacked QC figure and in S14.

## 50-nt control (p4m2, 50 nt; not in the manuscript)

The only panel kept from the 50-nt set: p4m2 coverage vs distance from the poly(A) site with every method reduced to one 50-nt read per fragment (BOBseq: read 2 truncated to 50 nt), showing that the flat BOBseq coverage does not depend on read length; the axis text states the 50-nt reduction.

## Placement

The preprint panels, their values tables and the sections above are mapped in `README.md` of this directory. The generators write panels and tables into the benchmark working directory; the repository ships the tables only, in `values/`, and `replot.py` redraws every panel from them. The expression and splicing panels (Fig. 6A, 6B, 6D, 7A, 7C to 7F, S5, S6A) are computed in `expression_splicing/`; the README of each analysis there describes its numbers.

## Style

Panels are plotted without legends, titles or notes; one shared legend per figure (drawn by `panel_legends.py`; `replot.py` puts the method legend inside each panel). Arial 7 pt. Colours (`benchmark/scripts/palette.py`, one source for every panel): DRUG-seq #A8325A, prime-seq #8E4E00, BOBseq #2E6B32. Composition classes: mRNA #4C9A4C, ribosomal-protein mRNA #6E6E6E, exonic other #9E9E9E, intronic #C6C6C6, intergenic #B5379B, rRNA #E6B422, mitochondrial #2260A9. The numbers in values/ are the reference; replot.py restyles any panel from them.
